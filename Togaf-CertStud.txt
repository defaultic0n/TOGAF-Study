Q: A sponsor has approved the start of an architecture engagement and wants a single signed document that fixes the scope of work, names and deliverables and the schedule, and commits the architecture team to deliver against named milestones. Which Phase A output satisfies this requirement?
S1: The Statement of Architecture Work, which is the contract between the architecture organization and the sponsor.
S2: The Architecture Vision, which sets the high-level aspiration but leaves scope and schedule to be fixed later
S3: The Business Transformation Readiness Assessment, which confirms the organization can absorb the change
S4: The Architecture Definition Document, which records the detailed basedline and target for every domain
A: S1
E: The Statement of Architecture Work is the Phase A deliverable that contracts the architecture team to the sponsor, fixing the scope, deliverables, schedule, and acceptance criteria for the engagement. 

Q: A supplier is about to start building a solution that must conform to the enterprise's target state. E: an architecture contract is the governance artefact that sets out, between the architecture function and the delivery party, the deliverables expected, the quality gates and the conformance obligations the work must satisfy

Q: A programme must specify, for a required customer-authentication capability, first the vendor-neutral specification that commits the enterprise to no particular product, and later the specific purchased product that actually implements it. Which two building block types, in order, does the programme need?
S1: Two Architecture Building Blocks, one for the specification and one for the product.
S2: An Architecture Building Block to define the capability, then a Solution Building Block to implement it.
S3: A single component that serves as both the specification and the product, so no second building block is needed.
S4: Two Solution Building Blocks, one for the vendor-neutral specification and one for the purchased product.
A: S2
E: Architecture Building Blocks (ABBs) are vendor-neutral specifications that define what a capability should do. They represent the "what" without specifying how it will be implemented.
Solution Building Blocks (SBBs) are specific implementations of ABBs, typically representing actual products or technologies that will implement the capability.

Q: While modelling the target Business Architecture for a bank, the team has collected a mixed list of statements. The team wants to record both groups correctly. How should they be treated?
S1: Record the hard boundaries as constraints on the design and keep the preferences as principles that guide choices.
S2: Record both as business drivers, since each one explains why the target Business Architecture has to change.
S3: Record the hard boundaries as risks, because breaching a retention period would expose the bank to enforcement.

A: S1
E: Record the hard boundaries as constraints on the design and keep the preferences as principles that guide choices.
